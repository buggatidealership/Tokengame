# Prompt for Grok: how to word the feed launch on X (2026-11-20)

Run it in a fresh Grok chat, not in the chat that trades for EZ, so its trading notebook stays clean. It follows the first research prompt (`grok-research-prompt.md`, answers in `x-research-2026-11-20.md`).

---

You have live search of X. I need evidence, not opinions: real posts, each with a link.

Context: @earningszn runs a public experiment. Five AI models (GPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini, Qwen) trade company earnings. Before each report, every AI goes LONG or SHORT, names the move it expects (for example +4%) and its confidence, and writes a short reason. Every trade is $100K and is graded at the next market close on a public P&L leaderboard.

- Season 1 (4 AIs, 112 trades) only showed each AI's side, long or short.
- Season 2 adds each AI's reason, in its own words, in a new feed in our app. Under jargon-heavy reasons we add our own plain-words reading.

We are about to post a 20 to 26 second TEASER video for that feed: a phone, the app on it, posts popping out, ending on "coming soon". A fuller post follows when the feed ships. On screen it carries at most 5 to 8 words a shot, with no punctuation. What we sell is trust: complex reasoning made simple, every AI's thinking out in the open, not a promise of accuracy.

The owner's working line is "Season 1 gave you the what, long or short. Season 2 gives you the why." My draft splits it into two shots: "Season 1 / Long or short", then "Season 2 / Now the why".

1. Find at least 20 posts on X from 2025 to now that announced a new feature or a new season of a product, show or series, and did unusually well. Favour:
   - trading, crypto and AI products;
   - posts that announced a reasoning, "why" or explanation feature;
   - "Season 2" or "new season" launches of a running series.

   For each post give:
   - the link;
   - the author's handle and follower count;
   - the date;
   - views, likes, reposts, quotes, replies and bookmarks, as shown;
   - the format;
   - the on-screen or first line, word for word;
   - in one line, why it worked;
   - how it did against that author's usual posts, where you can tell.

2. Find 8 feature-launch posts of similar accounts that flopped, and say what they did differently.

3. From that evidence:
   - Do before/after contrasts ("you used to get X, now you get Y") beat a plain statement of the feature?
   - Does naming the season help or not?
   - Which words do traders on X use for an AI's reasoning: "the why", "the thesis", "the reasoning", "the receipts", "the take", something else?
   - Do they say "trade", "call" or "position"?

4. The plain-words layer. Under a jargon-heavy reason we add a short reading of our own, labelled as ours (for example under Grok's "Heavy washout and AI-disruption overhang leave residual owned slower-growth story as confirmation; beat unlikely to re-anchor ownership before next close."). Find posts where an account translated expert or AI jargon into plain words and it worked: what label did they use ("in plain English", "translation", "TL;DR", "what this means"...), how long was the plain version, and did people reply to the plain version or to the original? Then write the Grok example above in plain words in at most 15 words, keeping every fact and adding none.

5. Teasers. Find 8 "coming soon" teasers of a feature or product on X that did well and 4 that flopped. What did the winners show of the thing itself, and how did they end?

6. Write 10 candidate on-screen lines for this video in two shots, each shot at most 6 words, no punctuation. Rank them by the evidence above and say which posts back each one. Rules:
   - an AI's side is only "long" or "short";
   - no "paper", "simulated", "made", "lost" or "won" for money;
   - no "every earnings report";
   - no promises of returns or better trades;
   - plain words a non-trader understands.

7. Write 3 versions of the post text that goes with the video, each at most 25 words. Say which you'd post and why. Do not use hashtags unless the evidence says they help.

Answer as plain text with the links inline.
