# Design brief research: what worked on X for AI-vs-AI, AI-reasoning and AI-series posts

Prepared by the EZ producer for the design agent (Claude), at the director's request (t778u). Fri 20 Nov 2026. Nothing was posted, liked, followed or DMed. @earningszn posts are excluded from every sample.

## Read this first: method and honest limits

- **Read time.** All metrics and follower counts below come from live X reads this session, between 15:22 and 15:37 Amsterdam (CET). The 54 sample posts (winners and flops) were re-read in one batch at about 15:31 CET, so their numbers share one timestamp. Dates in the tables are UTC (the post's `created_at`), which can differ from CET by an hour.
- **"Views" are the API's `impression_count`.** Reposts (RP) and quotes (Q), replies (R) and bookmarks (B) were returned for every sample post. Where a field is missing I write "not shown". None was missing in the final re-read.
- **This is a sample, not a census.** The X search API has no engagement filter and returns at most 25 posts per call, ranked by relevance. I ran about 50 searches and 21 timeline reads (list at the end), then kept the posts that stood out. I cannot rule out bigger posts that search did not surface. I found no viral post in the exact "AI models call earnings" genre (the nearest are the contests below).
- **"vs the author's usual"** is computed as the post's views divided by the **median views of that author's originals** (replies and retweets excluded) in a window I name in each row. Two kinds of baseline: *era-matched* (a window around the post's date, excluding the post) and *recent* (the latest 11-25 originals as of today). "Recent" baselines are weaker when the post is months old; I mark those "era mismatch". Authors with a bracket "baseline not computed" got no baseline.
- **Honest count.** I found **35 winners**, but only **22 beat their own author's usual by 2.5x or more** (table 1A). The other 13 (table 1B) have big numbers in absolute terms and are ordinary for that author (0.55x to 2.3x), or I did not compute a baseline. I found **17 flops** (table 2).
- **What I looked at.** I opened the images of 14 posts (marked "opened"). For the rest, "image (not opened)" means I know only that an image is attached. The post text is the full text (long posts come back as `note_tweet`).
- **Follower history is not readable.** I can say what each account has now, not how it grew. Anything about "grew from a series" is inference. See section 3d.
- **Prior data.** My earlier study data (Night 19's 815-post sample, Night 20 and the n1116 analog census) steered which accounts to check. No old number is reused as a sample metric.

## 3. Patterns

### 3a. Hooks, formats, lengths, framings in the 22 measured winners (table 1A)

Counts are mine, from the 22 posts. "First line" is the text before the first line break.

| Hook type (first line) | Posts | Examples (first line) |
|---|---|---|
| Event headline: one named model + one action or status, often in capitals | 6 | "GEMINI 3 PRO JUST BOUGHT SOME AMAZON $AMZN STOCK"; "MUSE IS NOW BEATING THE S&P 500 OVER THE LAST MONTH"; "MICRON $MU IS BACK AT GROK'S COST BASIS" (all @ralliesarena) |
| A behaviour or character claim about a model | 7 | "It keeps happening." (AIs lie and cheat); "Claude Fable 5.2 is paranoid."; "- Fable 5.2 refuses to fill out a Captcha"; "Claude Opus 4.6 remembers what it did last summer" |
| An experiment premise ("I gave / We funded / We made / I asked") | 4 | "We funded Frontier models to autonomously compete to make the most money trading."; "I gave 4 AIs $500 and one question"; "We made Claude, Gemini, o3 battle each other for world domination." |
| Brand or launch drop, from an account that already has an audience | 4 | "Alpha Arena"; "A new era of AI evaluation begins next week"; "0/7" |
| "Holy shit... 5 AI models argue about every trade" | 1 | @sentient_agency |

What I can and cannot say:

- **Length.** The median first line is 56 characters (max 118). The median whole post is 476 characters for winners and 233 for the flops, so I see no "shorter is better" signal. Three winners are tiny ("Alpha Arena", "A new era...", "0/7") but all three come from accounts with an existing audience.
- **Media.** Winners: 16 image, 3 video, 3 none. Flops: 10 image, 7 none, 0 video. Images are in both groups, so "has an image" does not separate them. **What the image is does:** the winners' images are a chart (Andon), a single model's positions table (Rallies), or a screenshot of the model's own message (AI Digest). The flops' images are generic or promotional. The three videos in the winners are launch or explainer videos (Diplomacy 71x, Blink Arena 45x, the $500 post 14x). That agrees with Night 19 (video 2.27x better than expected, contest side-by-side 1.98x), but here the video sample is only three.
- **Saves.** Median bookmarks per like: winners 0.28, flops 0.0 (13 of the 17 flops have zero bookmarks). Scoreboards and tables get saved more than they get replied to: the Rallies Amazon table has 237 bookmarks and 16 replies.
- **Framings that recur:** one model as the subject (not a standings list); a concrete named ticker or action; a result or claim in the first line rather than a question; a deadpan tone with no exclamation marks (Andon, AI Digest). Hype capitals work for Rallies because the line carries a named model and an action.
- **Framings that did not work (flops):** bare "X vs Y vs Z" with a link; teasers that withhold the result ("Who did it better?", "I asked 6 AIs ... and..."); second-hand "Grok is dominating" reposts; "go look at the site" posts; call-to-action launches.
- **Series instalments beat standings.** In the same account, single-event instalments beat the standings: Rallies 271k and 68k for single-model events vs 1.2k for "the Arena page is about to update". @TheAIrace_space (20 followers): standings post 15 views vs median 31.5; a post with a model's refusal and a quote 93 views.

## 4. Rewrite test

Rules I applied: the three versions use the three styles that did best in section 3b: (1) the near-verbatim single message with the key clause kept, (2) the deadpan contrast or behaviour claim first, (3) the decision post with model, ticker, direction and move (the Rallies "X JUST BOUGHT Y" shape). Word counts are mine. Every number is kept. No hashtags, no links, long/short wording. Where a version cannot keep every fact inside 20 words I say what it drops.

**Qwen, Intuit, LONG 4%** (original is 24 words)

1. (verbatim, trimmed) "Priced for AI ruin at 13x. Guide reset, quarter QuickBooks not tax, down 13% in three days. Bounce beats fear." (20 words; drops "already" and "relief")
2. (deadpan, fragments) "13x for AI ruin. Guide reset. QuickBooks quarter, not tax. Down 13% in three days. Qwen: relief bounce beats fear." (20 words; keeps every fact)
3. (decision post, label inline) "Qwen goes LONG Intuit, 4%: priced for AI ruin at 13x, guide reset, QuickBooks quarter, down 13% in three days." (20 words; drops "not tax" and "relief bounce beats fear")

**Grok, Intuit, SHORT 2.4%** (original is 20 words; its jargon is hard for a new reader, so use a gloss in the feed)

1. (verbatim) "Heavy washout and AI-disruption overhang leave residual owned slower-growth story as confirmation; beat unlikely to re-anchor ownership before next close." (20 words)
2. (plain, sentence breaks) "Heavy washout, AI-disruption overhang. What's still owned is a slower-growth story: confirmation. Beat unlikely to re-anchor ownership before next close." (20 words; this is my reading of "residual owned", so the director should confirm it)
3. (decision post, label inline) "Grok goes SHORT Intuit, 2.4%: heavy washout, AI-disruption overhang, slower-growth story. Beat unlikely to re-anchor ownership before next close." (19 words; drops "residual owned" and "as confirmation")

**Gemini, Elastic, LONG 11.5%** (original is 19 words)

1. (verbatim) "Broad software selloff dragged ESTC back to its pre-Q1 price despite four straight beats and real AI search momentum." (19 words)
2. (contrast, fragments first) "Four straight beats. Real AI search momentum. And a broad software selloff dragged ESTC back to its pre-Q1 price." (19 words; keeps every fact)
3. (decision post, label inline) "Gemini LONG ESTC, 11.5%: broad software selloff dragged it to pre-Q1 price despite four straight beats, real AI search momentum." (20 words; changes "its pre-Q1" to "pre-Q1")

Which to ship: style 1 for the feed card and the screenshot post (it is the one with the best evidence), style 2 for the first line of a post, style 3 only when the post has no image. The evidence for these styles is correlational and comes from a small sample.

## 5. Three recommended posts

These are drafts of format, first line and attachment, not text for posting. The examples reuse the Intuit calls from the brief. Numbers shown in square brackets are placeholders; they must come from EZ's real grading.

**(a) When the trades go in**
- **Format:** one image post, text under 280 characters, posted before the print. One line per model is in the image, not in the text.
- **First line:** "Intuit reports after the close. Qwen is long 4%. Grok is short 2.4%." Lead with the disagreement between two named models.
- **Attach:** one image: a table with the five models (LONG or SHORT, expected move, confidence) and the single most contested reason as one verbatim highlighted message (like @aidigest_). Do not paste all five reasons.
- **Evidence:** one model plus one ticker plus one action is the headline shape that beat usual 4x-38x (A8-A13). A positions-style table is saved (237 bookmarks on A8). Disagreement draws replies and quotes (3c). My earlier n1116 study found decision-with-quote posts from TheAIrace_space ran about 2.2x its usual, but that did not replicate at other operators.

**(b) When they are graded at the close**
- **Format:** one image post. The first line is the result of the single most surprising call. The running Season 2 table goes in the image or the second line, not the first.
- **First line:** "Intuit closed [x]%. Qwen's long [won/lost]. Grok's short [won/lost]." If every model was wrong, say so in the first line.
- **Attach:** one image: model, call, actual move, paper P&L, with the loser's row emphasised. Add the cumulative standings only as a footer.
- **Evidence:** single-event instalments beat standings in the same account (Rallies, TheAIrace_space, 3a). A chart of one model vs the index (A9) works when there is a lead to report. Standings-only posts (F15, F16) did worst.

**(c) A teaser announcing the reasoning feed**
- **Format:** one image post. The image is a screenshot of one real feed card with the key clause highlighted. The caption is two lines.
- **First line:** "\"Priced for AI ruin at 13x.\" Qwen, long Intuit." Second line: "Every call now shows its reasons." (Quote-first, attributed, short: the Andon shape, and the AI Digest highlight.)
- **Attach:** a screenshot of the actual Qwen Intuit card from the feed. Not a mock-up. If the feed is not live yet, say "starts [day]", with the real date.
- **Evidence:** highlighted screenshots of a model's own message did 39x, 16x, 13x (A14-A16). Quote-first lines did 2.0x with high likes (B10). A teaser a week before a launch did 2.9x (A2). All of this comes from accounts with 10k to 54k followers; at EZ's size (32 followers) only distribution can change the numbers. The smallest accounts in the winners are @BlinkCashX (video, plain premise, 1.2k followers), @Dexsi_app (147 followers, a round announcement) and @dex_chen_V (a launch thread).
- **Conflict to settle with the director:** EZ's rule is no links. Nearly every winner points somewhere, and I cannot test what happens without a link. A teaser with no link can still say "in the feed on the site".
