# Selling without selling — research for round 5 (2026-09-30)

Question from the owner: how do we get people to buy $EZ without saying "buy" louder? How do we walk someone
through what we do so it is simple to understand ("we know it's boring, but consistency is everything")?

Labels: **[sourced]** from a cited source · **[derived]** my inference for EZ · **[hypothesis]** untested, needs real traffic.

## What the research says

1. **Pressure backfires on people who know they are being sold to.** The Persuasion Knowledge Model (Friestad & Wright 1994)
   says people learn to spot persuasion tactics and discount them. Perceived sales pressure produces negative outcomes for the
   seller. [sourced] Crypto buyers from X are some of the most "persuasion literate" people online: every day they see shills. [derived]
2. **Showing the work makes people value it more.** "Operational transparency" (Buell & Norton; Buell, Kim & Tsay): when customers can
   see the work being done, they perceive more effort, value the service more, and trust it more. In one field study, reported quality went up 22%.
   People who got photos of the government fixing their request filed 60% more requests. [sourced]
3. **People believe their own conclusions more than ours.** Self-persuasion (Aronson; Müller and colleagues): asking people to come up with
   the argument themselves beats handing them the argument. It creates no reactance, and the effects last longer. [sourced]
4. **Doing something small makes people value it more.** IKEA effect (Norton, Mochon & Ariely 2012): people value what they helped make,
   but only when they finish the task. [sourced] A finished "make your own call" is the EZ version. [derived]
5. **Step by step helps experience more than recall.** Scrollytelling studies: better engagement and lower cognitive load than plain text.
   Comprehension is about equal, and the effect on trust was small and inconclusive. [sourced] So a journey helps people stay and feel it is clear;
   it does not by itself make them trust us. The record does that. [derived]
6. **Why people buy memecoins.** FOMO, social proof and the joke or culture of the coin lead the surveys (Kraken: 29% chase short-term gains;
   23% like the cultural or satirical side). [sourced] EZ should not borrow FOMO tactics (fake timers, "last chance"). The real,
   honest urgency is the earnings calendar: the next print has a real date. [derived]
7. **Trust era.** (From RESEARCH-hooks.md) Buyers screen for things they can check, like the contract, public numbers and never-edited records. A public live number is
   the marketing (the Hyperliquid pattern). [sourced]

## What this means for the three variations

- **Say "buy" once, at the end, quietly.** Everything before that earns it: the routine, the receipts, the misses shown as prominently
  as the wins. [derived from 1, 2]
- **Show the machine working.** Dates, the prompt shape, the grade and the self-review loop, as a routine that repeats on a schedule.
  The boring repetition *is* the proof. [derived from 2]
- **Let the visitor reach the conclusion.** Ask questions instead of making claims ("Who would you back next?"). Let them call the next
  print themselves (stored only on their device) before they see what the machines called. [derived from 3, 4]
- **Honest urgency only.** The next print date and the lock time come from the sheet. No invented scarcity. [derived from 6]
- **One idea per screen on mobile.** Most traffic comes from X on phones. [sourced in RESEARCH-hooks.md]

## What would prove this wrong
- If people who make their own call don't click through to Get $EZ more than people who don't, self-persuasion isn't converting here.
- If the quiet single CTA gets far fewer clicks than the old page with the same traffic, subtle is too subtle for this audience.
- If time on page goes up but return visits around print dates don't, the journey entertains without building the habit.
- Shared assumption across all three: visitors arrive curious, not already sold. If most arrive already wanting to buy (e.g. from a
  friend's shill), every one of these pages slows them down, and the buy button should move to the top.
