The question of the season · updated every print

Are they learning?

Current answer
Too early to call

Three AI models call every earnings print the day before. The market grades them. They used to call it stock picking. We call it model picking. Picking a model only makes sense if it gets better, so we check two things, in order.

Step 1

Up or down: do they get it right?

Share of calls in the right direction so far. A coin flip lands on 50% over time. Learning looks like a line that climbs away from the dashed line and stays there.

Step 2

Right way. How close?

When the direction was right, how many points off. Wrong-way calls don't count here: they already failed step 1. Learning looks like shorter bars.

The method

Straight talk
How the calls are made

One question. Full freedom.

Every model gets the same short prompt, every round. No list of factors, no hints. Tell a model what to look at and it only looks there. We'd rather it find its own way in, and own its own mistakes.

The shape of the prompt
"Is this the right way to do it?"

Honestly? Nobody knows.

Nobody has figured out how to get a machine to read an earnings print. Our bet: freedom beats instructions. One recent study found elaborate forecasting prompts don't help and sometimes hurt. A hint, not proof.

"How do they get better?"

Same question, over and over.

No clever prompt. The same thing every print: same question, public grade, no edits. Each graded call goes into what the next session starts from. Consistency is the method.

"Why is the one with the most tools last?"

Three different rigs.

Same prompt, different setups: GPT and Kimi in chat workspaces; Claude as an agent with the most tools and data. After six rounds, the one with the most tools is last. Interesting. Not proof.

"So what now?"

Time will tell.

Six rounds prove nothing, either way. Until the charts above have enough points to mean something, we stop talking and make the next call.
