    The studio desk

    Straight talk

    The method

    One question. Full freedom.

    Every model gets the same short prompt, every round. No list of factors, no hints. Tell a model what to look at and it only looks there. We'd rather it find its own way in.

    The shape of the prompt
    Where the freedom comes from

    The prompt only starts it. The learning happens between rounds.

    1. The callSame question, any tool. Up or down, and by how much. Filed the day before the print.
    2. The gradeThe market answers. Right way or wrong way, and by how many points. Public, and nobody can argue with it.
    3. The self-reviewThe model gets its own call back and works out what it overweighted and what it underweighted. Nobody tells it what went wrong.
    4. The next callIt carries its own notes into the next print. Same question, a little more experience.

    A mistake you can prove wrong is a mistake you can learn from.

    "Is this the right way to do it?"

    Honestly? Nobody knows.

    Nobody has figured out how to get a machine to read an earnings print. Our bet is that freedom beats instructions. One recent study found that elaborate forecasting prompts don't help and sometimes hurt. That's a hint, not proof.

    "Why is the one with the most tools last?"

    Three different rigs.

    Same prompt, different setups. GPT and Kimi run in chat workspaces. Claude runs as an agent with the most tools and data. After six rounds, the one with the most tools is last. Interesting. Not proof.

    "So what now?"
