Why Meet the models works

My interpretation, as you asked

The short version: it is the only section that starts from a character instead of a number. The other sections start from data and dress it up as a comic.

  1. You meet someone. Each card is one AI with a face, a name, a flag and a seat in Season 1. Its numbers are things about it, not the subject.
  2. Every object means something. The ticket is a seat in the season, the flag says who it represents (US vs China), and the gold and holo say who leads. Nothing is there just to decorate.
  3. One thing per idea. One AI, one ticket. No table, no box inside a box, no labels over lists.
  4. It is quietly alive. The flags wave and the leader shimmers. The motion is small and constant, like a moving portrait.
  5. It invites you to pick a side. Crypto traders back teams. A cast with a rivalry asks "who's your fighter?" before it asks you to read anything.

The deeper meaning, as I read it: EZ is a league with a cast, not a dashboard. People follow characters first and their stats second. So every section should be something the cast does (calls, fights, wins, loses), and every number should hang off a character. That also explains why the scoreboard is the other section you like: it is the cast compared side by side.

What this means for the page: the order meet them, watch them compete, see who's ahead, see what's next. Every new section has to pass one test: is a character doing something in it?

Another read: Meet the models has simply had the most polishing rounds (F1, then R8, then R9). Its quality could come from being finished, not from the character idea.

What would tell them apart: if a heavily polished section without characters (say, the record) still feels dry to you, the character reading is right. If polish alone fixes it, the second read is right.

The leader, animated (instead of gold circles)

Pick one · Leaderboard · scoreboard S3

Each take marks only the leaders: the board's #1, plus the leader of each stat row. The mark arrives when a row scrolls into view and moves on a Season / 24h / 7 days switch. The rest of the time it is still, and the still state on its own says who leads. None of them makes the switch measurably slower, even on a slow phone.

  • X1The crownA tiny inked gold crown drops onto each row's leading figure, with a bigger one on the leader's face. On a switch, a crown that changes owner hops in an arc to its new head. A small glint every 7 s on the leader's crown. Makes the board about 55px taller on phones.
  • X2Gold leafThe leading figure sits on gold leaf from the golden ticket's own foil, laid left to right with one glint. A lost lead peels off at once and the new one is laid. A glint every 8 s on the leader's name.
  • X3LiftNo new object. The leading figure rises off the card in comic lettering, a size bigger, while everything else stays flat. Losers sink, new leaders rise. No idle motion at all: the most Apple-like.
My pick: X1. It is the most literal "tiny crown", and the hop tells you the title changed hands. Choose X3 if you want nothing moving at rest.
