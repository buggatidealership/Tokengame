## (a) Summary

Three findings change how tonight's pieces should be built. First, **branding early works, but a logo laid over the picture does not do the work by itself**: TikTok and System1 (887 ads, 92,000 viewers, June 2025) found that ads with the highest early brand recognition got 92% more brand-awareness lift, yet logo overlays *reduced* lift while logos *inside the scene* raised it, and Meta and Toluna (May to August 2026) found a brand shown several times beat a single end-frame logo by 1.8x. So the house bug in the corner is a series signature, not the branding; the EZ. mark has to appear in the middle of the picture, on the phone, the card or the ticket, two or three times. Second, **showing an AI's reasoning raises trust whether the AI is right or wrong** (CHI 2025, 308 people; a 2026 preprint calls traces "persuasive but not informative"), while **a confidence number and the observed record calibrate trust** (FAT* 2020; CHI 2019), and **context, not source, is what makes a plain-words note trusted** (Community Notes experiment, PNAS Nexus 2024). The feed earns lasting trust only if every reason sits beside its graded outcome, losses as loud as wins, with the disagreement between models in view, because inconsistency between explanations cuts over-reliance on wrong answers. Third, **seasonal identities that work keep the mark and change the world around it**: UEFA's 2024 Champions League identity remade the starball in glass and re-recorded the anthem but left the logo "unchanged"; every Olympic Games gets its own "Look of the Games" over the fixed rings; Tropicana's 2009 redesign, which dropped its distinctive assets, lost 20% of unit sales in seven weeks and was reversed; committed fans judge logo changes harshly (632 people, 2010). The research also contradicts two lines in the brief: the house bug's wordmark "in the piece's own face" throws away the logotype, which is among the most ownable brand assets (Ehrenberg-Bass, 1,281 elements, 2020), and a reasoning feed is not new on its own (Public.com shut its social feed in June 2025 because "this exact content is created by AI"); what nobody else has is the graded receipt.

## (b) Findings

### 1. Branding in short social video

**F1.1 Early brand recognition lifts brand outcomes on TikTok; logo overlays do not.** MEASURED. System1 and TikTok, "The Long & Short (Form) of It": 887 short-form ads, 92,000+ TikTok viewers, eight markets, matched to 350+ brand and conversion lift studies. Ads with the highest early brand recognition showed 88% higher memory lift, 92% higher brand-awareness lift and 85% higher brand-image lift than those with the lowest. But "logos in the first 2 seconds have been conditioned to be swiped past", "logos are the most powerful asset for driving recognition but have one of the highest costs in terms of attention", and the recommendation is "logos in context, which are integrated into the scene". System1 blog, 23 Jun 2025: https://system1group.com/blog/how-to-make-entertaining-short-form-ads-for-tiktok-social
- Write-up of the same study (Chief Marketer, 3 Aug 2025): logos in context +182% brand-awareness lift, fluent characters +57%, sonic assets in the first two seconds +191%, and "logo overlays reduced brand awareness lift" (no figure given). https://chiefmarketer.com/system1-tiktok-study-branding-elements-and-creator-led-content-improve-short-form-video-ads/
- B&T, 15 Oct 2025, quoting the study team: "put your logo in the first few seconds and be done with it" became "lazy branding" that viewers learned to scroll past; a logo on a product "goes from being this after thought to something that's actually integrated into the story". Fluent characters +67% (the write-ups differ: 57% vs 67%). https://www.bandt.com.au/attention-is-skippable-system1-reveals-how-to-harness-the-magic-of-tiktok/
- A further secondary claim, "a static logo on the opening frame = +50% skipping", appears only in a search summary I could not trace to a page. Not used.

**F1.2 Brand and message in the first five seconds, several brand moments, not a single end frame.** MEASURED (small sample). Meta and Toluna: about 100 ads, 100 viewers each, Germany, UK, Italy, Turkey, 65+ creative variables per ad, top 20% vs average. Ads that set up both brand and core message in the first five seconds were 1.7x more likely to drive strong purchase intent; "dynamic branding that appears multiple times" was 1.8x more likely to perform strongly than a single end-frame logo, which underperformed; for lower-funnel ads, branding under about 25% of the runtime did better (how a persistent corner logo was coded is not stated). Audio plus visual cues beat visuals alone. Toluna write-up, 4 Aug 2026 (findings first discussed on Meta's podcast in May 2026): https://tolunacorporate.com/what-makes-short-form-video-ads-work-meta-toluna-study/

**F1.3 In feeds, attention comes in short bursts, so the brand goes early.** MEASURED. Meta and Realeyes, "Decoding Digital Video Environments": 16,835 ad impressions, 6,076 people, camera-measured attention, simulated Reels, TikTok, Shorts and Stories placements, fieldwork 2022. Feed and short-form produced about 0.16 to 0.17 brand recallers per second of attention against about 0.07 in stream; brand recognition overall was comparable across environments; the advice for feed is to introduce the brand "in the first few seconds". Report hosted by IAB, Jul to Aug 2023: https://www.iab.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Meta-Realeyes-Decoding-Digital-Video-Environments.pdf (the per-second figures are from summaries of the report: secondary).

**F1.4 Google's ABCD: "Brand early, often, and richly".** MEASURED by a vendor for the platform (treat as vendor-reported). Google's guidance: "Introduce your brand or product from the start and maintain that presence"; "Audio brand mentions enhance onscreen brand visuals' performance"; "Use a variety of branding elements". The claimed effect: ads that follow the ABCDs show up to a 30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and 17% in long-term brand contribution (Google/Kantar, "The Short & the Long of ABCDs Effectiveness", April 2021; Kantar scored about 11,000 ads on 180 features with its Link AI model, so the lift is model-predicted, not observed sales). https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551 and https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence
- Google's YouTube playbook adds a "five-second test": a human face, "say and show your brand in the first five seconds", title cards that work with the sound off. HYPOTHESIS (guidance). https://business.google.com/ca-en/think/search-and-video/youtube-marketing-success-playbook/

**F1.5 Brand placed at the centre survives fast skimming; brand at the edge does not.** MEASURED. Brasel and Gips, "Breaking Through Fast-Forwarding", Journal of Marketing 72(6), Nov 2008 (two eye-tracking studies and a choice study): fast-forwarded ads with brand information at screen centre still built brand memory with 95% of the frames gone and no audio; brand information elsewhere added little. https://www.msi.org/working-papers/breaking-through-fastforwarding-brand-information-and-visual-attention/ ; summary https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/11/081103102314.htm . Older; that thumb-scrolling behaves like fast-forwarding is DERIVED, not measured.

**F1.6 Older platform figures on the first seconds.** MEASURED by Facebook with Nielsen, secondary only: up to 47% of a video campaign's value was delivered in the first three seconds and up to 74% in the first ten (Smart Insights, 2016); viewers were 23% more likely to remember a brand named in the first three seconds (The Drum, 25 Jun 2018). https://www.thedrum.com/news/2018/06/25/social-media-video-tips-the-art-standing-out-crowded-feeds ; https://smartinsights.com/?p=83170 . Old and secondary: direction only.

**F1.7 Logos and logotypes are the most ownable brand elements; colour is among the least.** MEASURED. Ward, Yang, Romaniuk and Beal, "Building a unique brand identity", Journal of Brand Management 27(4), 2020: 1,281 in-market elements, 13 categories, 19 countries. Characters, logos and logotypes have the greatest potential for unique ownership; colours, taglines and on-pack imagery the least (WARC summary). https://link.springer.com/10.1057/s41262-020-00187-6 ; https://www.warc.com/content/feed/characters-logos-are-brand-elements-with-greatest-ownership-potential/en-GB/1826
- Romaniuk's grid scores each asset on **fame** (share of category buyers who link it to the brand) and **uniqueness** (share who link it to this brand only). Building Distinctive Brand Assets, OUP 2018. The often-quoted "50% fame and 50% uniqueness" threshold appears only in a practitioner summary; I could not confirm it in her text. https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/distinctive-brand-assets-framework-ehrenberg-bass/

**F1.8 Sound off.** MEASURED, old. Facebook's internal tests (2016) found captions raised average video view time by 12%; publishers reported 50 to 85% of their Facebook video views silent (Digiday, 2016). Verizon Media and Publicis (2019, US survey): 92% watch mobile video with sound off at least sometimes. No 2024 to 2026 figure for X turned up; the often-quoted "80% of Reels watched with sound on" has no primary source I could find. https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/ ; https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/captions-increase-viewership-for-facebook-video-ads/ . X autoplays video muted (platform behaviour). DERIVED: on X, every fact must read with the sound off; the sound is a second channel.

**F1.9 Persistent corner bugs.** Weak evidence. TV practice (the station "bug") is universal, but I found only one controlled study, a small classroom experiment (Pescatore, UNC) reported second-hand: a continuous bug helped station recall, a brief one did not. I could not open it. HYPOTHESIS: a fixed corner mark is a series signature that teaches "this is EZ" across many posts; it is not proof of brand lift inside one video, and F1.1 and F1.5 say it does little on its own.

**F1.10 (v2) Text overlays help music-led reels; speech helps most; a slower start does not hurt.** MEASURED (sample size not stated). Emplifi, brand Facebook Reels, 19 Mar 2026: text overlays raised 3-second retention 5.1% and 10-second 7.6% across all reels, and for music-based reels 4% at 3 s and 9.8% at 30 s; speech in the first 3 s raised 3-second retention 11.7% and 10-second 24.7% against music-only; a slower start raised 30-second retention 7.7%, and "pacing shows minimal impact on viewer retention"; seamless loops in reels up to 7 s raised replays 18.7% and reach 23.6%. https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/ . DERIVED: EZ's films are music plus text, the format where overlays help; there is no evidence that faster cutting itself keeps viewers (see open question 8).

**F1.11 (v2) The first picture decides.** MEASURED by Netflix: consumer research in early 2014 found artwork was the biggest influence on a member's decision to watch and "over 82% of their focus while browsing". https://about.netflix.com/en/news/the-power-of-a-picture (the often-quoted "1.8 seconds per title" is secondary only). DERIVED: frame 0 on X, which is all a viewer with autoplay off sees, deserves poster-level design.

**F1.12 (v2) Animated logos are not automatically better.** MEASURED. Brasel and Hagtvedt, "Living brands: consumer responses to animated brand logos", Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science 44(5), 2016 (five studies, 638 adults): the effect of animating a logo depends on what the brand stands for; press releases describe the fit in opposite ways, so I do not cite the direction. https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/joamsc/v44y2016i5d10.1007_s11747-015-0449-2.html . DERIVED: keep the EZ. mark itself still in the house bug; let the world around it move.

### 2. Feed products around trading and AI reasoning

**F2.1 Robinhood Social: posts must carry a real, verified trade that updates itself.** MEASURED only as a rollout figure. Announced 10 Sep 2025 at HOOD Summit: "live, verified trades and authentic profiles", posts must include actual trades, positions update automatically, trade from the feed, follow traders. Q1 2026 results (28 Apr 2026): beta to 10,000 customers "with strong early engagement" (no figure). Later opened to all eligible US customers (Finance Magnates). https://robinhood.com/newsroom/hood-summit-2025-news ; https://www.axios.com/2025/09/10/reddit-x-robinhood-social-meme-stock ; https://www.financialcontent.com/article/gnwcq-2026-4-28-robinhood-reports-first-quarter-2026-results ; https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/robinhood-lets-all-eligible-us-customers-share-their-trades-and-portfolios/

**F2.2 Robinhood Cortex Digests: plain-English "why it moved".** MEASURED by the company. Digests summarise "why a particular stock may be moving in plain English"; "used by hundreds of thousands of Robinhood customers", 95% of surveyed users positive (UK launch release, 19 Aug 2025). https://robinhood.com/newsroom/digests-by-robinhood-cortex-uk . On X, Robinhood's "why" taglines underperformed (Grok X research, feed teaser F4 0.43x, O7 0.90x).

**F2.3 Public.com shut its social feed because AI now writes that content.** MEASURED (company statement). "Sunsetting Social", reported 5 Jun 2025: the feed's core content, earnings-call recaps and daily market updates, "Now, this exact content is created by AI", faster and more accurate. https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/public-shuts-down-social-trading/ . DERIVED: commentary alone is a commodity in 2026; EZ's difference is that each AI's reasoning is pinned before the report and graded in dollars after it.

**F2.4 Kalshi Social: Feed, Following, Leaderboard; most users never trade.** Product facts from Kalshi's help centre (undated): three views, Feed / Following / Leaderboard; comments, threaded replies, GIFs, likes, bookmarks, shares; profiles with a nickname changeable once every 30 days; Kalshi moderates. https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823843-what-is-kalshi-social . Kalshi says about three quarters of its users have never deposited (Finance Magnates, 5 Aug 2026), using it "to track prices rather than trade them". https://www.financemagnates.com/fintech/75-of-kalshi-users-never-trade-but-platform-still-intends-to-capitalise-on-that/ . DERIVED: a prediction product's audience is mostly spectators, which is EZ's audience too.

**F2.5 Polymarket comments: a few people write almost everything, and comment sections get gamed.** MEASURED. A quantitative description of Polymarket, May 2023 to Sep 2025: 778,634 wallets, 30.5M trades, 384,586 comments, 571,523 reactions; comments are highly concentrated (one summary: the top 10% of commenters wrote 76.4%; check the paper). Journal of Quantitative Description, 2026: https://journalqd.org/article/view/9814 . Abuse reports (secondary, single sources): fake data releases posted in a market's comments; scammers holding both sides to stay visible under the "Holders" filter. https://www.webpronews.com/polymarkets-social-feed-experiment-is-turning-a-prediction-market-into-a-misinformation-machine/ . DERIVED: one-tap reactions reach the silent majority; open comments add moderation cost and a misinformation surface.

**F2.6 eToro: following is built around a track record.** Product facts: CopyTrader, Popular Investors, a News Feed where users follow investors; CopyTrader came to the US on 29 Oct 2025. Popular Investors were 3,300+ at 31 Dec 2024, about 0.1% of funded accounts (prospectus, via secondary summaries). https://www.etoro.com/en-us/news-and-analysis/latest-news/press-release/etoro-brings-copytrader-to-the-u-s-empowering-investors-to-trade-smarter/ . No feed-engagement figures are published.

**F2.7 Seeing others' success raises risk-taking, more so with a copy button.** MEASURED (lab). Apesteguia, Oechssler and Weidenholzer, "Copy Trading", Management Science, 2020: information on others' success significantly increased risk-taking, and more when subjects could copy directly. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3508 . DERIVED: a P&L leaderboard plus "follow this trade" pushes viewers toward risk; EZ should not add a copy action to the feed (there is no legal read yet either, CLAUDE.md, 3 Nov).

**F2.8 Nof1 Alpha Arena: every model's outputs public, with a chat log per decision.** Product facts, secondary only (alphaarena.ai returned 429 tonight; nof1.ai's blog 404): Season 1 ran 17 or 18 Oct to 3 Nov 2025, six models with $10,000 each on Hyperliquid, "all model outputs and corresponding trades made public" (Decrypt, 20 Oct 2025); an expandable "chat log" per model with prompts, reasoning and timestamps, e.g. "12/03 21:50:56: Entering long AMZN to recover 57% drawdown", a leaderboard sorted by account value (review dated 16 Jan 2026, figures as of 4 Dec 2025). https://decrypt.co/345006/ai-crypto-trading-showdown-deepseek-grok-winning-gemini-implodes ; https://www.onedayadvisor.com/2025/12/nof1ai-alpha-arena-review-season-15.html . What travelled on X was the founder's and CZ's posts about who was winning, not the chat logs (Decrypt; Grok X research A1, A2). DERIVED: the log is the proof layer; the leaderboard and single events are what people share.

**F2.9 Community Notes: a plain written explanation is trusted more than a label, and it changes behaviour.** MEASURED.
- Drolsbach, Solovev and Pröllochs, preregistered experiment, PNAS Nexus 3(7), 2024: community notes were rated significantly more trustworthy than context-free flags across the political spectrum, and the gain "primarily stemmed from the context provided" (the explanation), not from trust in the community. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38948016/
- Slaughter, Peytavin, Ugander and Saveski, PNAS, Sep 2025: after a note was attached, reposts fell 46.1%, likes 44.1%, replies 21.9%, views 13.5% (over a post's whole life: 11.6% fewer reposts). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478135/
- **(v2)** Solovev and Pröllochs, arXiv 2503.10560, 13 Mar 2025, 8+ months of X notes: the odds a note is rated helpful are 2.70x higher when it links to an external source; notes citing highly biased sources are rated less helpful. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10560
- DERIVED: EZ's "In plain words" reading works like a note: its power is the explanation, so it must be exactly faithful (the brief's rule), visibly ours, and visibly tied to its source, the AI's own words it reads.

**F2.10 Explanations raise reliance on AI answers whether they are right or wrong; inconsistencies cut it.** MEASURED.
- Kim et al., CHI 2025, preregistered, 308 participants: explanations increased reliance on both correct and incorrect LLM answers; when explanations contained inconsistencies, over-reliance on incorrect answers fell. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554
- Palod, Biswas and Kambhampati, preprint dated 15 May 2026: reasoning traces and post-hoc explanations "are persuasive but not informative", raising acceptance regardless of correctness. https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.10930 (not yet peer reviewed).
- DERIVED: a reasoning feed sells confidence, not truth. Trust that lasts needs the receipt beside the reason (graded P&L, right or wrong) and the disagreement between models kept in view.

**F2.11 How the reasoning apps show thinking.** Product facts. OpenAI moved ChatGPT's o3-mini to fuller reasoning summaries in Feb 2025, saying users told it seeing the reasoning helps them decide and builds trust; the raw chain of thought stays hidden (TechCrunch, 6 Feb 2025). https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/06/openai-now-reveals-more-of-its-o3-mini-models-thought-process . A July 2026 report says the collapsible thinking block stopped showing by default (unconfirmed by OpenAI). https://startupfortune.com/chatgpt-users-report-the-thinking-phase-has-disappeared-by-default-and-founders-building-on-openais-apis-should-understand-exactly-what-changed-and-why/ . HYPOTHESIS: the pattern is a short summary first, the long trace one tap away.

**F2.12 Rivalry and moderate controversy drive conversation.** MEASURED.
- Chen and Berger, "When, Why, and How Controversy Causes Conversation", JCR 40(3), 2013: controversy raises the chance of discussion at low to moderate levels and lowers it beyond that. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:doi:10.1086/671465
- Rathje, Van Bavel and van der Linden, PNAS, 2021: in 2.7M posts, each word about the out-group raised the odds of a share by 67% on average. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8256037 . Hostility drives that; EZ wants rivalry, not animosity.
- Grok X research: disagreement between named models drew replies and quotes (3c); "5 AI models argue about every trade" 50x (A7, era mismatch).

**F2.13 Notifications and streaks: small causal lifts, mostly measured by the companies themselves.** MEASURED.
- Bidargaddi et al., JMIR mHealth 2018, micro-randomized, 1,255 users, 89 days: a tailored push made app use in the next 24 hours 3.9% more likely (RR 1.039, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.08). https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123
- Duolingo's own A/B tests: Streak Wager raised Day-7 retention by 14%; splitting the streak from the daily goal raised Day-14 retention 3.3% (company blogs). https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak
- DERIVED: a notification at the two moments of suspense (calls are in; the close) is the defensible use; more is noise.

**F2.14 Predicting before the outcome raises curiosity.** MEASURED (lab, attitudes only). International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction, 2024: fans who predicted outcomes before watching highlights reported more curiosity and flow and rated the viewing more positively. https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/10447318.2024.2327178 . No study found on return visits.

**F2.15 X's ranking weights.** Already in this repo (CLAUDE.md, the X post research of 28 to 29 Oct): copy-link share 20, reply 5, quote 5, follow 4, like about 0.5 to 0.7, video watch weights 0 by default. Not redone.

**F2.16 (v2) A confidence number and the observed record calibrate trust; numeric uncertainty costs little.** MEASURED.
- Zhang, Liao and Bellamy, FAT* 2020 (two experiments): showing a confidence score per prediction helped people calibrate their trust; local explanations did not, and calibrated trust alone did not always improve decisions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02114
- Yin, Wortman Vaughan and Wallach, CHI 2019: trust follows a model's stated accuracy, but its effect shrinks once people see the model's observed accuracy. https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=569874
- van der Bles et al., PNAS 2020, five experiments incl. a BBC News field test, 5,780 people: numeric uncertainty barely lowered trust in a number and not in its source; verbal hedges lowered it more. https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/304699
- DERIVED: "58% confident" on the post row is the right kind of number (it calibrates); the model's graded record beside its name (hit rate, P&L, W-L) is what people will trust over any claim; vague hedging words in our plain line would cost trust.

### 3. Seasons that shift style but keep identity

**F3.1 UEFA Champions League, 2024 "Kick of Light": the mark stays, the world turns to glass.** Product fact (UEFA, 23 Aug 2024): the starball and the stadium dome "are now made of glass", "the colours move playfully around the edges of the stars", the anthem was re-recorded with refined orchestration, and "iconic elements such as the logo remain unchanged". https://uefa.com/news-media/news/0290-1badd36e73b2-c3e5755b7fae-1000--uefa-unveils-new-uefa-champions-league-brand-identity . The 2018 identity before it (DesignStudio) kept the dark-blue palette, added accent colours and a lighter cut of the Champions typeface. https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/4-10-june-2018/designstudio-gives-uefa-champions-league-a-new-illuminated-look/

**F3.2 The Olympics: fixed rings, a new "Look of the Games" every edition.** Product fact: each Organising Committee designs its own emblem, typefaces, colours, pictograms and materials over the unchanged rings; pictograms have been redrawn by every host since Tokyo 1964. https://www.theolympicdesign.com/olympic-games/the-look-of-the-games/ ; https://olympics.com/ioc/olympic-brand

**F3.3 Stranger Things: one logotype, a new treatment each season.** Product fact: the title keeps its modified ITC Benguiat letterforms; Season 2 added a big numeral, Season 3 block letters outlined in red, Season 4 a translucent outline. https://www.printmag.com/culturally-related-design/stranger-things-logo-design/ ; https://fabrikbrands.com/stranger-things-logo-exploring-the-stranger-things-symbol/ (secondary; dates differ between sources).

**F3.4 Spotify Wrapped: a new design system every year under a fixed name.** Product fact: explorations start each June; 2022 monograms, 2023 "a decided departure" with no grid, 2024 vivid colour and lively motion, 2025 a "visual mixtape". https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/spotify-wrapped-campaign-identity-2023-graphic-design-301123 ; https://newsroom.spotify.com/tag/brand-design/

**F3.5 Premier League: the lion simplified, then a "kit of parts".** Product fact: the 2016 identity by DesignStudio kept the lion, simplified to its face, with a sans-serif and a five-colour palette; the 2023 update by Nomad kept the essentials and changed through "everyday iteration". https://www.dezeen.com/2016/08/15/uk-premier-league-football-logo-lion-minimal-rebrand-designstudio/ ; https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/14-august-18-august-2023/premier-league-identity-update-nomad/

**F3.6 NBA City Edition: a new uniform each season, the franchise marks recognisable.** Product fact: Nike and the NBA release a new City Edition set each season (2025-26 is the ninth), each "a departure from the core brand", often remixing a team's own past. https://www.nba.com/news/2025-26-nike-city-edition-uniforms-unveiled ; https://www.nba.com/news/behind-the-design-2022-23-city-edition-uniforms

**F3.7 Fortnite: every season gets its own logo and key art, revealed with a name and a date.** Product fact: e.g. Chapter 7 Season 4's logo was posted on X with the release date, its pixel style signalling the theme. https://beebom.com/fortnite-chapter-7-season-4-override-announced/ . The Fortnite wordmark itself stays.

**F3.8 Formula 1: small yearly changes to the broadcast graphics draw complaints.** Product fact: for 2025 the fastest-lap purple was lightened and some fonts changed to align with TAG Heuer; fans complained in pre-season testing (secondary). https://www.si.com/onsi/f1/news/f1-fans-rip-apart-2025-graphics-at-pre-season-testing-such-a-shame-01jn19jk806c . F1's director of broadcast called 2026 "a different look" (SVG, 28 Jul 2026). https://www.sportsvideo.org/2026/07/28/a-new-way-to-watch-how-formula-1-and-sky-sports-are-pushing-race-coverage-into-a-new-era/

**F3.9 Dropping distinctive assets costs sales.** MEASURED. Tropicana's redesign of 8 Jan 2009 (the orange with a straw removed): unit sales fell 20% and dollar sales 19% (about $33M) between 1 Jan and 22 Feb 2009 per IRI; PepsiCo reversed it on 23 Feb. https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2009-04-02/tropicana-fiasco-from-arnell-is-gift-that-keeps-giving

**F3.10 (v2) Committed fans judge logo changes harshly; tell them first.** MEASURED. Walsh, Winterich and Mittal, Journal of Product & Brand Management 19(2), 2010, field experiment, 632 people, two shoe brands: after a logo redesign, brand attitude fell among strongly committed consumers and rose among weakly committed ones, more so the bigger the change; the authors advise involving and notifying the most committed before a public reveal. https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/do-logo-redesigns-help-or-hurt-your-brand-the-role-of-brand-commi/ ; https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/starbucks-effect-committed-customers-dont-logo-redesigns . DERIVED: EZ's current followers (about 32 at the Grok X read of 20 Nov) are its committed core; the Season 2 switch must not touch the mark, and they should see the new look announced before they meet it.

### 4. How agencies run creative development

**F4.1 Account planning (strategy) started in 1968.** Stephen King at JWT and Stanley Pollitt at BMP set up planning departments in 1968, putting the consumer researcher inside the campaign team. https://ipa.co.uk/about/our-history/ad-fest-100/adland-s-gamechangers/adland-s-gamechangers/stanley-pollitt/ ; https://www.warc.com/Content/Article/Point_of_view_Plannings_genesis/103979

**F4.2 The brief carries one single-minded proposition.** Practice: one sentence with the single most important thing the work should convey, pressure-tested by strategist and creative director; "a brief with two propositions produces work with no proposition". HYPOTHESIS (practice). https://app.manifest.ly/public/checklists/creative-brief-checklist

**F4.3 Emotion and fame beat rational messaging on long-term effects.** MEASURED. Binet and Field, The Long and the Short of It (IPA, 2013): 996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories from the IPA Effectiveness Databank; emotional campaigns about twice as efficient as rational; campaigns with very large salience (fame) effects nearly twice as likely to deliver both short-term sales and long-term share growth; short-term response metrics are a poor guide to long-term success. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation ; https://www.thinkbox.tv/research/thinkbox-research/the-long-and-the-short-of-it

**F4.4 Pre-testing as a gate.** Vendor-reported. System1's Star Rating claims 250+ validations; adding it to share of voice raised explained market-share growth from 27% to 48%; of ads tested, 48% score 1 star and 1% score 5 stars. https://system1group.com/methodology . HYPOTHESIS for our use: our numeric checks are tonight's pre-test.

**F4.5 (v2) The creative team and the tissue meeting.** Product history. Bill Bernbach paired a copywriter with an art director (Phyllis Robinson and Bob Gage, first at Grey, then at Doyle Dane Bernbach from 1949), the team model agencies still use. https://campaignbrief.com/?p=136856 ; https://www.commarts.com/features/doyle-dane-bernbach . The "tissue meeting" shows a client rough ideas, drawn on tracing tissue in the pre-computer days, before any is developed; credited to Jay Chiat, brought to London by HHCL in the early 1990s. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_meeting ; https://www.thedrum.com/insight/2023/08/25/tissue-sessions-can-help-fend-cheaper-rivals-just-ask-crispin-porter-bogusky

**F4.6 (v2) Briefs are where the work goes wrong.** MEASURED (survey, with a commercial interest). The BetterBriefs Project, 2021, 1,700+ marketers and agency staff in 60 countries: 80% of marketers think they write good briefs, 10% of agencies agree; 78% of marketers think their briefs give clear strategic direction, 5% of agencies agree; respondents estimated up to a third of budgets wasted on poor briefs and misdirected work. https://ipa.co.uk/news/betterbriefs ; https://www.marketingweek.com/best-practice-guide-better-briefs/ . ## (c) Implications for EZ

### (v2) The insight and the propositions, for the desks

- **Insight.** People do not trust AI predictions, and an explanation alone makes them trust an AI more whether it is right or wrong (F2.10). What they can trust is a reason written before the result, graded after it in dollars, in public, next to four other AIs who took the other side (F2.1, F2.9, F2.16). Nobody else shows that: commentary is free now (F2.3); the receipt is not.
- **Single-minded proposition for the feed** (internal wording): every AI's reason is written before the report and graded at the close. On screen, two shots, no punctuation: "Same report five AIs disagree" / "Read why then see who was right". (Built on the Grok feed teaser research's first-ranked line, plus the receipt.)
- **For the outcome video:** one close, every AI's side and the P&L it produced. Single-event instalments beat standings in the same accounts (Grok X research 3a).
- **For the prediction video:** same report, opposite sides, each AI's reason in one line. Disagreement draws replies (F2.12).
- **For the season switch:** same league, new season. The mark and the meaning stay; the world changes (F3.1, F3.2).

### Rules for the Season 1 / Season 2 switch on the website

Each rule is checkable.

1. **Fixed across seasons: the EZ. mark and its place.** Same SVG geometry, same top-left position, same size in both seasons (pixel diff of the mark's box = 0 apart from anti-aliasing). Every working example keeps its mark: UEFA, the Olympics, Stranger Things, Spotify, Fortnite; committed fans punish mark changes. (F3.1 to F3.7, F3.10)
2. **Fixed: the logotype "Earnings Zeason".** One set of letterforms in both seasons. Season 2 may change its material (glass, paint) the way Stranger Things changed outline and fill, never its letters. Logotypes are among the most ownable assets. (F1.7, F3.3)
3. **Fixed: the meaning colours.** Green = LONG and P&L above zero, red = SHORT and below, gold = the leader. Each token within ΔE 10 of Season 1 (the brief's check). Colour is weak as an identity asset, so it is not what carries the brand; it carries meaning, which must not move. (F1.7)
4. **Fixed: the data grammar.** "LONG (+4%)", "58% confident", "P&L +$12,790", the round and company order. A visitor who switches seasons reads the same sentence shapes.
5. **Changes: everything that is world, not mark.** Typefaces other than the logotype, the grid, component shapes, materials (Season 1 paper and tickets, Season 2 glass over paint), motion grammar, pictograms, sound bed. The Olympic model: a full "look" per edition over a fixed core. Check: Season 2 differs in at least 5 of these 7. (F3.2, F3.4)
6. **The switch shows the mark staying put while the world changes around it.** The transition keeps the logo still for its whole duration (its box moves 0 px) while the page behind it changes. This is how UEFA and Fortnite announce: the known mark in a new world. (F3.1, F3.7)
7. **Season 1 stays one tap away and unchanged.** The switch is a labelled control at the top (both labels visible), defaults to the current season, remembers the visitor's choice; the Season 1 record stays exactly as published. Tropicana shows the cost of taking away what people knew. (F3.9)
8. **A season change is clearly a new look, announced first.** F1's minor 2025 tweaks drew complaints; whole new looks with a name and a date (Fortnite, Wrapped) are events; committed fans should hear before they see. (F3.7, F3.8, F3.10)

### Branding rules for videos (renderer-checkable)

1. **B1 Brand in the scene by 2.0 s.** The EZ. mark or the logotype appears *inside* the shot's main object (the app header on the phone, the glass card, the ticket), with its centre inside the middle 60% of the frame's width and height, by t = 2.0 s. (F1.1, F1.5)
2. **B2 Brand and message by 5.0 s.** The piece's single-minded line is on screen by t = 5.0 s. (F1.2, F1.4)
3. **B3 Three brand moments, not one end frame.** At least 3 separate in-scene appearances of the mark or logotype (each visible ≥ 0.5 s, starts ≥ 3 s apart), the end card counting as one at most. (F1.2)
4. **B4 The corner bug is a signature, not the branding.** Keep the house bug (top-left, constant, the mark still), but it does not count toward B1 or B3. (F1.1, F1.9, F1.12)
5. **B5 Frame 0 carries brand and premise.** Viewers who turn autoplay off see only the first frame (repo research, 12 Nov), and the picture decides the click (F1.11). Check: frame 0 contains the mark or logotype and at least one premise word.
6. **B6 Sound off reads complete.** Every fact on screen as text; nothing told only by voice or sound. (F1.8, F1.10)
7. **B7 One sonic mark, always the same.** The same 1 to 2 s EZ sound (a sting or a bell) inside the first 2 s of every film, on top of whatever bed. Sonic assets in the first 2 s gave the biggest awareness lift in the TikTok study; on X most views start muted, so it never replaces B1. Check: cross-correlation with the reference sting in 0 to 2 s ≥ 0.7. (F1.1, F1.4)
8. **B8 One logotype.** "Earnings Zeason" in the same lettering in every film; the piece's own face is for everything else. (F1.7) This changes the brief's house bug, which sets the wordmark "in the piece's own face".
9. **B9 The end card holds ≥ 1.5 s (brief) but is not the main brand moment.** (F1.2)
10. **B10 No static logo-only opening.** The first second shows the story's object with the brand on it, not a logo card. (F1.1)

### What not to do

- Lead with "the why" or with the season name ("Season 2 / Now the why"): self-contrast and season-name hooks underperformed on X (Grok feed teaser research F1, F4, F5).
- Show a reason without its outcome, or bury wrong calls (F2.10).
- Paste reasoning as a jargon wall without the plain line (Grok feed teaser research F3, 0.39x).
- Rely on a corner logo or an end card for branding (F1.1, F1.2).
- Re-letter the logotype per film or per season (F1.7, F3.3).
- Add a copy or "follow this trade" action next to a P&L leaderboard (F2.7).
- Open comments at launch without moderation (F2.5).
- Change small things each season (a lighter purple, a bolder font) and call it a new season (F3.8).
- **(v2) Cite these, which do not hold up:** "consistent branding raises revenue 23% / 33%" (Lucidpress and Demand Metric, self-reported estimates by brand staff, 2016 and 2019); "85% of video is watched without sound" for X (2016 Facebook publishers); "80% of Reels are watched with sound on" (no primary source); "50% fame and uniqueness" thresholds (not found in Romaniuk's text); "a static logo opening frame means +50% skipping" (untraceable).

## (d) Open questions only the owner or X data can answer

1. **The house bug's wordmark:** keep "Earnings Zeason" in one fixed lettering everywhere (my recommendation, F1.7), or in each piece's own face (the brief)?
2. **A sonic mark:** do we want one EZ sound (a bell, a sting) on every film? Nobody here can hear, so the owner picks it.
3. **Sound on X:** what share of @earningszn video views play with sound? If X's analytics cannot say, treat every film as silent first.
4. **Notifications:** the feed lives on the website; alerts need web push or X. Which does the owner want, if any?
5. **Comments:** none at launch (my recommendation), reactions only, or full replies?
6. **Season 2 sound:** a new bed family for Season 2, or Season 1's beds re-recorded (UEFA re-recorded its anthem rather than replacing it)?
7. **X data the owner can pull:** which EZ posts produced follows (weight 4), copy-link shares (weight 20) and replies; whether in-scene logo films beat bug-only films on profile visits.
8. **(v2) Pace:** the brief asks for a new shot every 1.5 s or less ("exciting"); the only recent retention data (Emplifi, Mar 2026) found a slower start lifted 30-second retention 7.7% and "pacing shows minimal impact". Keep the fast grammar as the owner's taste, or test one film with a held first 2 s?
9. **(v2) A voice:** speech in the first 3 s gave the largest retention lift in the same data (+11.7% at 3 s, +24.7% at 10 s); EZ's films are music and text only. Does the owner want a spoken line (his own voice, or none)?
10. **(v2) Telling the core first:** should the current followers see the Season 2 look (a post, a pinned preview) before the site switches (F3.10)?
