## Rules kept
- **True marks and logos.** The AI marks are drawn from `MODELS[m].d` (the site's own paths) inside the painted object (struck, pressed, enamelled, painted or cut as a finish); the company logo is the site's own file (`SE.logo`), multiplied into the face (a dark plate for logos made for dark grounds). No mark or logo is generated by a model, so none can be misspelt. Checked at 3x: GPT, Claude, Grok, Kimi and Apple read in every take.
- **Words and numbers are page text.** Hedra produced blank objects only (prompts end "No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no symbols, no icons"); I checked every cut-out at full size: none carries a letter or a sign.
- **Live data.** Clock hands = New York time to the second; the red pip = 4 PM; "Bell in" = time to the next close that grades calls (Monday 2 Nov, 4 PM ET, with none locked on a Saturday); No. = `ROUNDS.length` (80); golden ticket = P&L leader (`SE.lead`, Grok); step 1 = the biggest company reporting next (Apple) with "?" balloons until calls are in. `window.__kCheck` holds what each shot used.
- Step 4 reads "Every call stays on the public record".
- 320, 390 and 1280: no sideways scroll, no page errors. The only element past the edge is the film bill's `.vb-k`, already on the live page.

## How each could fail
- **K1:** the tallest first screen (the clock row between the step rows): only steps 4 and 1 sit above a 390x844 fold. Brass everywhere is one hue: the coins read alike at a glance (the marks are struck, not coloured). The turning tubes may read as a loading spinner (60 s per turn).
- **K2:** the hero ticket pushes the loop below the fold on phones; the leader's name appears twice (hero and step 3). The red wax seals in Starring look alike until you read the gilded marks. Desktop leaves the right column taller than the left.
- **K3:** the denim ground is busy behind the small pins; enamel cream faces next to cream tapes make one beige band. The alarm-clock pin is small (64px), so its hands are hard to read. The jacket is a fashion object; it may read as merch rather than a competition.
- **K4:** the most playful, and the most likely to read as gambling (a midway). The striker loop runs forever; bulb arrows blink (both off with reduced motion). Chalk "80" on the scoreboard is page text in a bold face, not chalk.
- **K5:** the calmest and most readable, but cut paper is the furthest from the films' look and from "3D first, comic finish" (the owner's pick A). The scalloped labels are heavy around small words.
- **All:** the balloons are drawn shapes (not painted); the headline is still page lettering with CSS materials, not a painted title. The step pictures are small on phones (about 40 to 70 px per object): the craft shows at 3x and on desktop more than at arm's length. Safari not checked (border-image with fill, background-clip:text). Cut-outs are keyed from chroma green: a faint green fringe can show at 3x on dark grounds.

## Recommendation
K1 if the clock is kept as the hero (it carries "live" by itself and is the most finished object); K3 if the owner wants the most "handmade" feel. Next step either way: paint the headline and the balloons (the last two drawn pieces) and give the steps more room on phones.
