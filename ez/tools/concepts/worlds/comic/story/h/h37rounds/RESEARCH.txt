H37 ROUNDS: how fine art and top collectibles are presented, and what it means for "Every round so far"
Research done 2026-11-02 (web search; dates are the source's own where it has one, "undated" where it has none; vendor pages are marked as such).
The owner's ask: the Mag 7 shown as the prized pieces, every other round in the same world, nothing cheap, the tap-to-lift tag kept.

=====================================================================
1. MUSEUM HANGING: single line for what matters, salon for the many
=====================================================================
Principle: today's museums hang the important works in one row at eye level with space around each; the salon hang (frames
above and below eye level, edge to edge) is used on purpose when the point is abundance, the collection as a whole.
Sources:
- Polk Museum of Art, "The Salon Style Hang" (essay, undated): "today's trend in museums is to exhibit works of art in single
  rows with plenty of space around each piece"; the salon hang = "works hung higher and lower than eye level and in large groups".
  https://PolkMuseumofArt.org/media/salon-style-essay
- Canton Museum of Art (exhibition note, undated): normally single rows, salon style brought back for one exhibit on purpose.
  https://www.cantonart.org/node/3039
- ArtsHub, "How to get a salon hang right" (undated): https://www.artshub.co.uk/news/career-advice/how-to-get-a-salon-hang-right-2555174/
- The 57-inch (145 cm) centre line used by galleries is a practitioners' convention, not an institutional standard (e.g. Eden
  Gallery, Chairish, 2020s; no museum publication found that sets it). https://eden-gallery.com/news/how-high-should-you-hang-art-on-a-wall
What it means here: two hangs, two meanings. The Mag 7 hang in one line, at one size, each with room (take A's honour line). The
other rounds can be a salon: dense, edge to edge, and that density itself says "a big record". A salon is arranged around an
anchor piece; ours can be each week's best grade (a stated rule, from the sheet), so size carries a fact, not decoration.
Watch: a single line of 7 does not fit a 390px phone at a readable size. Either it scrolls sideways (A) or it breaks into a hero +
a row (B).

=====================================================================
2. ONE FOCAL POINT: the corridor that ends on one work
=====================================================================
Principle: the Rijksmuseum's Gallery of Honour is "an extended corridor directed towards a clear focal point" (the Night Watch);
side alcoves were once curtained so the eye went to the far end. Cuypers' 1885 plan, kept in the 2013 reopening.
Sources:
- Rijksmuseum, "The Gallery of Honour" (museum page, current, read 2026-11-02): https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/visit/inside-the-museum/gallery-of-honour
- Rijksmuseum, "10 things about the Great Hall, Gallery of Honour, and Night Watch Gallery" (undated):
  https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/stories/10-things/story/great-hall-gallery-of-honour-night-watch-gallery
What it means here: the section needs one thing the eye lands on first, not seven equals in a cabinet. Today the case gives each
Mag 7 slab ~90px on a phone, smaller than every other round below it: the prized pieces read smallest. Takes B (the newest Mag 7
alone in the tall bay) and A (each Mag 7 bigger than anything below it, under its own light) fix that inversion.

=====================================================================
3. LIGHT MAKES THE HIERARCHY: accent ratios
=====================================================================
Principle: lighting designers set the object brighter than its surround by a ratio: about 3:1 to separate, 5:1 for a clear focal
point, 10:1 for drama; museums get there by dimming the room rather than brightening the object (conservation).
Sources:
- BD Online, Mark Ridler (BDP Lighting) on the V&A's fashion galleries (undated interview): roughly 3:1 in the gentler gallery,
  about 10:1 for a dramatic installation. https://www.bdonline.co.uk/mark-ridler-of-bdp-lighting-on-the-vandas-fashion-galleries-and-working-with-rafael-vinoly/5031416.article
- ERCO / Energylight, "Lighting for Museums" (manufacturer guide, undated): https://energylight.net/assets/Lighting-for-Museums-ERCO-Energylight.pdf
- Lighting Design Lab reference (undated): 5:1 distinct focal area, 10:1 minimum strong accent, 15:1 dramatic.
  https://www.lightingdesignlab.com/node/820
- EN 12464-1 (2011) contrast categories 1:1 / 3:1 / 10:1, as cited by an ICONARP paper: https://iconarp.ktun.edu.tr/index.php/iconarp/article/view/266/228
- Goppion (museum case maker; vendor catalogue on ArchDaily, undated): light placed inside the case, LED or fibre, no UV/IR.
  https://www.archdaily.com/catalog/en/products/19516/display-case-lighting-goppion
What it means here: the Mag 7 get a light of their own (picture light, downlight in the bay, a spot over each stand, a lit top
shelf) and the archive sits in ambient light. The page's gold is reserved for section titles, so the "light" is warm light in the
painting, never gold type. The light belongs to the painted world (lamp, bay, shelf), not a CSS glow on its own (lesson 80).

=====================================================================
4. LABELS: the tombstone, at the front, readable
=====================================================================
Principle: each work gets a short "tombstone" label (who, what, when, medium); labels go at the front of a case at a comfortable
height, never on the case's back wall; minimum sizes are large (NMI: 18pt minimum for wall labels).
Sources:
- National Museum of Ireland, Exhibition Guidelines: Labels (PDF, 2020): https://museum.ie/getmedia/055b4f4a-5b1a-41ba-b30b-143e998d818a/Exhibition-Guidelines-Labels.pdf
- Western Australian Museum, "Text and labels in museum exhibitions" (undated): in showcases text at the front, at a comfortable
  reading height. https://museum.wa.gov.au/research/development-service/text-and-labels-museum-exhibitions
- Museums & Galleries NSW, "Exhibition labelling" (PDF, dated 2026-05, not opened): https://mgnsw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Exhibition-labelling.pdf
What it means here: the slab label already is the tombstone (company, ticker, grade). Two rules follow: the label must be readable
at the size we draw the slab (today's small slabs fail: name and grade word collide), and the facts that do not fit (who won, the
move) go in a front label that appears on demand: our lift tag is exactly a tombstone that comes forward when you lean in. Take A
also writes the Mag 7's tombstone on the wall in cream lettering (vinyl wall text is how museums letter labels on a wall).

=====================================================================
5. AUCTION HOUSES: the lot as a numbered, documented object
=====================================================================
Principle: every lot is a number, a picture and a fixed block (artist, title, date, details, estimate); marks next to the lot
number carry status; online catalogues add video per lot and long-form essays for the stars.
Sources:
- Sotheby's online catalogue article, "Five New Ways to Experience Sotheby's New York Marquee Auctions" (undated): "enhanced
  imagery, video for every lot". https://www.sothebys.com/en/articles/how-to-experience-sothebys-marquee-sales-this-june?locale=en
- Sotheby's press release (2020): new e-catalogue "featuring enhanced visual storytelling".
  https://sothebys.com/en/press/sothebys-shifts-select-march-april-sales-to-online-auctions
- Christie's Conditions of Sale (PDFs dated 2026-04-23 New York and 2026-05-07 Hong Kong): symbols beside the lot number carry
  status (e.g. no reserve). https://www.christies.com.cn/pdf/Live/LiveConditionsOfSale-NewYork-23APR26.pdf
What it means here: our rounds already are lots: a number (R80), a film (video per lot), a grade, a fixed label. Keep the round
number findable (the tag, the album page header "Rounds 71-80"), keep one block format everywhere (one component per thing).
Not used: estimates and prices (no money claims on rounds; the guardrails).

6. GRADED CARDS: how serious collectors and sellers show slabs
=====================================================================
Principle: collectors show graded cards as SETS (completion is the game) and give each slab its own space; serious shows and
sellers put the headline cards upright on stands under light and the many flat in a glass counter.
Sources:
- PSA Set Registry (psacard.com, read 2026-11-02; ~335,000 registered sets): sets are ranked by completion and grade; the
  non-competitive "Collectors Showcase" is for cards outside a set; annual awards with engraved plaques.
  https://www.psacard.com/PSASetRegistry/ and https://www.psacard.com/PSASetRegistry/Information
- Sports Collectors Daily (2015): PSA's Digital Card Album "designed to look like a traditional trading card binder".
  https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/psa-launches-online-card-album-for-registry-sets/
- Phantom Display, "Tips for displaying your PSA graded cards" (vendor blog, undated): "Each card deserves its own space.
  Overcrowding can make your display look cluttered." https://phantomdisplay.com/blogs/blog/tips-for-displaying-your-psa-graded-cards
- Geek Vibes Nation, best sports card display cases (guide, October 2025): UV wall frames, LED-lit acrylic, lockable desk stands.
  https://geekvibesnation.com/best-sports-card-display-case/
- Avaria/Dyverse, National Card Show 2025 guide (not an official PSA source): PSA's booth shows the highest-graded Registry cards.
  https://avaria.dyverse.com/?p=21318
- Goldin (cardlines.com review, undated; Sports Illustrated on a Goldin Elite lot, undated): three auction tiers (Marketplace, Weekly, Elite), the Elite lots featured.
  https://www.si.com/collectibles/auctions/lebron-james-03-04-ultimate-collection-bgs-10-rookie-autograph-takes-center-stage-at-goldin-s-latest-elite-auction
  No Goldin or PWCC photography or listing guideline was found; not claimed.
What it means here: (a) the Mag 7 are a SET, and a set has a count: "5 of 7" is a true, checkable fact (take C) and it makes the
two empty pockets (NVIDIA, Apple) read as "still to come" rather than broken slots; (b) never let a slab be cut off or stacked
behind another (the owner's circled box: two films half sunk in an empty box); every slab whole, with its own room; (c) the tiers
(Elite vs weekly) are our Mag 7 vs every other round: featured up top, the rest in the same world below (takes D and E).

7. WATCH BOUTIQUES AND JEWELLERS: one piece per vitrine, the rest in drawers
=====================================================================
Principle: high-end boutiques show few pieces, each in its own lit niche, like a gallery; the stock lives in trays and drawers the
jeweller pulls out on request.
Sources (all vendor marketing pages; treat as practice, not standard):
- DG Display, "Why do luxury watch boutiques need a more restrained and well organized display space" (undated) and "2025 new
  trends in high-end watch store space design" (2025): clear rules for "how many pieces should be displayed per module", flagship
  displays "designed to mimic art galleries where every piece is curated".
  https://www.degreefurniture.com/why-do-luxury-watch-boutiques-need-a-more-restrained-and-well-organized-display-space.html
  https://www.degreefurniture.com/dg-display-showcase-2025-new-trends-in-high-end-watch-store-space-design.html
- DG Display on jewellery case lighting (undated): hide the light sources, no glare, light layered from above.
What it means here: take B. The newest Mag 7 alone in a tall bay, the others one per small bay, each lit; under the case a
cabinet, a drawer per week with its brass card holder, the newest drawer pulled open. "Drawer per week" scales: at 116 rounds it
is ~12 drawers, all visible as closed fronts, each one tap away.

=====================================================================
8. BROWSING A BIG COLLECTION WITHOUT LOOKING CHEAP: the archive's own furniture
=====================================================================
Principle: archives make many objects browsable through furniture that groups and labels them: Solander boxes and flat files in
print rooms, drawers with label holders in card catalogues (Dewey standardised card and drawer sizes in 1876), and now visible
storage, where the racks themselves are the display (V&A East Storehouse, opened to the public in May 2025: 250,000+ objects on open racks,
"pallets not plinths", and an "Order an Object" service to see one up close).
Sources:
- The Art Newspaper, "Pallets not plinths: the V&A opens its vast storehouse to the public" (2025-05-28):
  https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/05/28/pallets-not-plinths-the-va-opens-its-vast-storehouse-to-the-public
- Dezeen, "V&A East Storehouse by Diller Scofidio + Renfro" (2025-05-28): https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/diller-scofidio-renfro-va-east-storehouse/amp/
- Diller Scofidio + Renfro project page (undated): https://dsrny.com/project/v-and-a-east-storehouse
- Solander box (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-02): a clamshell box that opens flat, in print rooms for 250+ years. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solander_box
- Southwest Solutions, museum flat files (vendor, undated): steel cabinets of 4 to 15 shallow drawers.
  https://www.southwestsolutions.com/divisions/museums/museum-flat-files/
- Library of Congress blog, "New Book: Card Catalog's History" (2017-04): https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2017/04/new-book-card-catalogs-history/
What it means here: the cheapness the owner saw is a box with no structure: ten slabs sunk into a black slab, half of them cut off,
an empty dark bottom. Archive furniture gives the many a structure that is itself handsome: labelled groups (week = drawer, shelf,
page or row), brass label holders, every object whole. Grouping by week also answers "is this still going?" (the newest group is
this week) and it survives the season growing to 116 (lesson 44).

9. WHAT THE RESEARCH DOES NOT SETTLE (for the owner)
=====================================================================
- No source compares these displays for comprehension; the takes are design judgement on top of practice, not measured.
- "Prized" can mean newest (B's hero), complete set (C), or all equal in one line (A). The record does not say which; it is a
  story choice.
- Phones: museums design for walking past at 57 inches; a phone is 390px of thumb-scroll. Every take had to pick what gives:
  the single line scrolls (A), the hero shrinks the others (B), the set is small (C), the stands repeat today's size (D, E).
