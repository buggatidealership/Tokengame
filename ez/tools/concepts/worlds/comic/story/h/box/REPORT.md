# Round H10 · box: the first screen as one continuous scene (roughs)

Box office → the loop, learned by doing → the cinema, shown in the two styles the owner asked for.

**Boards**
- `ez/x2p-x3p-h10-box-390.png`
- `ez/x2p-x3p-h10-box-1280.png`

Each board starts with today's live X3+ first screen. Then, for each style:
- the path (cover, taking the ticket, the ticket in hand, the cinema doors, the doors opening, the film on the screen);
- the four objects closed (each with its "touch me" motion named);
- the same objects opened;
- close-ups at 2x.

**Video:** `ez/x2p-x3p-h10-box-path.mp4` (390x844, 87 s, 4.2 MB). It shows the whole path in style 1, then in style 2, in this order:
1. the cover held with the ticket nudging;
2. scrolling takes the ticket (the Hedra clip, scrubbed by scroll);
3. each of the four objects is tapped (its clip plays and the fact appears);
4. scrolling opens the cinema doors (a clip scrubbed by scroll).

**Cost:** $4.76 of the $10 budget.
- Paintings: $1.93 for 37 gpt-image-2 jobs at 1K/medium.
- Clips: $2.84 for 12 Hedra video jobs.
- 3 video jobs were rejected before they ran, at no cost.

Every job is listed in the table at the end, and every prompt is in `log.jsonl`.

## How to run it

1. Serve with `node serve.js /home/user/Tokengame/ez 8971`. Use this, not `python3 -m http.server`: python's server has no Range support, so a browser cannot seek a video and the scroll-scrubbed clips would freeze on frame 0. Cloudflare Pages does answer Range requests with 206, so production is fine.
2. Open the page: `box.html?s=ink|logo&o=a|b|c`, where `o` picks the object for step 4.
3. Shots and checks: `node shot.js <dir> ink,logo 390,1280 a,b,c`. Then make the boards with `/usr/bin/python3 board.py <dir> <out390> <out1280>`, and the live reference shot with `node live.js <dir>`.
4. Video: `node rec.js <out.mp4> ink,logo a`.
5. Paintings and clips:
   - `python3 mkjobs.py 1|2 > jobsN.json` writes the jobs (pass 1 paints each object's first state; pass 2 edits that image into its second state, using it as the reference so the object and framing stay the same);
   - `python3 gen.py jobsN.json` runs them. gen.py is cover6's runner, extended so a job's `start`/`end` files become `start_image`/`end_image` for a video model.
   - `jobsv*.json` are the clip jobs.
   - Raw clips land in `vid/` (not committed: 36 MB). The web copies in `web/` (960 px wide, no audio, crf 27, a keyframe every 4 frames so scrubbing seeks fast) are 0.6 to 2.4 MB each.

## What the page does

**One scene, three beats, everything live.** Every word and number is page text computed from `ez-data.js` and placed on a surface the painting left blank for it. Positions are % of the painting, so the text stays on its surface at any width, and each piece is fitted to its surface.

The full list, with each surface's name and the rendered size, is in `inv-ink-390.txt` and `inv-logo-390.txt` (regenerated by shot.js for every style, width and option).

1. **The box office.** The marquee is the masthead; there is no second masthead plate (cover6's D repeated it).
   - Its four tracks read: "Season 1 · No. 80" / "4 AIs trade earnings" / "Who calls it best?" / "80 rounds graded · next bell Nov 3".
   - The booth glass reads "Box office · take a ticket ▾".
   - The golden ticket in the slot is cut from the painting and nudges every 2.4 s. It is the one moving thing (lesson 86).
   - A tap on it, or scrolling, takes it: the Kling clip (box office → push-in → the ticket filling the frame) is scrubbed by scroll position, so the transition is the world's own move, not a code wipe (lesson 96).
2. **The loop, learned by doing.** Four objects, each with its own small motion: the seal wiggles, the bell sways, the ticket tilts, and for step 4 the vault wheel turns, the stamp bobs or poster 1 flickers.
   - A tap (or Enter/Space; each object is a role=button) plays that object's Hedra clip from its closed state to its open state. Then the fact appears on the blank surface the open painting carries.
   - With reduced motion the clip is skipped and it cross-fades.
   - The objects and facts (newest graded round, R80):
     - **(a) the sealed letter** "Exxon Mobil (XOM) · sealed Oct 30" opens to a black card with four ruled rows: GPT ▲2.5%, Kimi ▲1.5%, Claude ▼1%, Grok ▼1.3%;
     - **(b) the closing bell** rings, and the slate under it reads "XOM closed ▲2.17% / 2 of 4 right · GPT closest";
     - **(c) the golden ticket** turns over, and its back's name plate reads "GROK", with its panels "P&L +$148,480" (flat $100K per call) and "W-L 45-27", and "#1" on the stub;
     - **(d) step 4, three options, as asked:**
       - **A the vault door** opens a crack onto "Bet #1 NVIDIA", engraved in its brass plate;
       - **B the betting slip**, slid under the grille and stamped "#1's call" in the red ring, with "Bet #1 · NVIDIA" on the slip (on the sign above the grille in style 2);
       - **C coming attractions** (mine): three poster frames; frame 1 lights up with "Bet #1 NVIDIA · now showing", and frames 2 and 3 stay dark with "Bet #2 · Season 2" and "Bet #3". It is the only option whose picture says "the first of many" without a sentence.
   - Each step's line, in the owner's words, is lettered in the Overview's one heading face (Bangers gold with an ink stroke, tilted -2°) as the panel's caption.
3. **The cinema.** Scrolling scrubs the Vidu clip of the padded doors opening onto the auditorium.
   - The explainer film (`media/ez-landing-v4.mp4`, poster `-poster-ez.jpg`) sits on the painted screen.
   - "Now playing · How it works · 1:20" is on the blank sign over the doorway.
   - "Next: Meet the four models ▾" is in the right-hand poster frame.
   - Then the next chapter's heading, "Meet the models".

**No founders and no faces.** The model names appear only as data: the predictions, the slate, the ticket.

**The two styles.** Both use the same objects and words; only the painting and the lettering's medium change.
- **Style 1, the vault's brush and ink:**
  - heavy blacks, dry brush, rain, gold light, small green and red spots;
  - lettering in each surface's own medium: bulb letters on the marquee, engraving on brass, white paint on the black card, chalk on the slate, gold foil on the ticket's back, a red stamp.
- **Style 2, the logo's own style:**
  - a flat badge on near-black, the logo's cream, gold, green and red, hard edges, slanted shapes, speed slashes;
  - every word set in Kanit black italic (the nearest free face to the logo's wordmark).
- The first pass painted some blank surfaces cream (vault face, slip panels, marquee). These were repainted dark with a gold rim, because a pale ground holding text breaks rule 5.

## The Hedra video test (the brief's question)

**Yes, it works.** An image-to-video clip can start on our painted frame and end on the next one, and play as the transition: muted, and scrubbed by scroll or played on tap. Two models take both a `start_image` and an `end_image`, and both did it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kling-v3 | 3 s, 720p, standard, no audio | $0.252 | 70 s | 3.8 to 5.1 MB | 0.6 to 1.0 MB |
| vidu-q3 | 3 s, 720p, turbo | $0.231 | 43 to 57 s | 1.4 to 4.0 MB | 0.6 to 2.4 MB |

**How close the clips land on our frames:** I measured SSIM between each clip's first and last frames and the paintings given as start and end.

| Clip | First frame | Last frame |
|---|---|---|
| cinema, ink, vidu | 0.91 | 0.96 |
| cinema, logo, vidu | 0.97 | 0.98 |
| letter, ink, vidu | 0.85 | 0.91 |
| cinema, ink, kling | 0.90 | 0.88 |
| cover, ink, kling | 0.89 | (end painting is 1:1, the clip 16:9; it ends on the ticket filling the frame as asked) |

So the cut from the painting to the clip and back is barely visible. The page cross-fades the painted end state in as the clip ends.

**Other findings:**
- More clips also ran (letter, bell and ticket in both styles; vault in ink; the cover in style 2).
- vidu-q3 refuses `aspect_ratio` when a start image is given (400 INVALID_ARGUMENT); it takes the frame's shape.
- Other models in the catalogue that accept both frames: seedance-15-pro, veo-31, wan-2-7 (from 2 s), kling-o1, ltx-2-5 (from 6 s). Not tried.

**What went wrong:**
- **The style-2 cover clip drifts.** Mid-clip, the ticket loses its scallops and turns into a plainer ticket (visible at "scroll: the clip takes it" on the board). The flat style gives the model little to hold on to. A re-run with a 16:9 end frame of the same ticket would likely fix it.
- **Interpolation softens the ink.** In the brush-and-ink clips the dry-brush texture smears slightly mid-move; the end frames are clean.
- **Fallback:** the painted two-state cross-fade, which already runs under reduced motion and for the three objects without a clip (style 2's vault, the slip and the posters in both styles).

## My pick

**Style 1 (the vault's ink) with step 4 = C, coming attractions.**

**Style 1:**
- It is the bet chapter's own look, so the first screen and the last chapter read as one book (lessons 76, 88).
- Its surfaces take lettering in their own media (bulbs, engraving, chalk, foil), which is what makes the words look made by the scene and not pasted on.
- Style 2 is crisp and on brand, but each object reads as an icon rather than a place. The badge look suits the header and stamps more than a whole scene.

**Option C:**
- Its picture alone says Bet #1 is the first of many (a lit frame and two dark ones), which is the owner's reason for "Bet #1: NVIDIA".
- A, the vault, is the strongest object, but it spends the bet chapter's vault reveal on the first screen (lesson 87: show a reveal where the story introduces it).
- B needs reading ("#1's call" on a stamp).

## How each could fail

**All:**
- **These are 1K roughs.** At 1280 the cover and the cinema are soft. The pick needs a 2K paint with every surface sized for its words:
  - the slip's "Bet #1 · NVIDIA" is 8.8px at 390 in ink;
  - the envelope's line is 8.8px at 390 in style 2;
  - the vault plate's engraving is small and low-contrast at 390.
- **The step captions** (Bangers, in the gutter above each panel) are the one piece of lettering not on a painted surface. They follow the Overview's heading rule, but by lesson 85 they could read as laid on. Paint a caption plate into each panel, or put the step line on the object (the card's top row, the slate's first line).
- **The fitted text follows the painted surfaces only by eye.** Positions were read from a 5% grid. The rotated card rows (style 2 letter) and the tilted ticket panels sit within about a degree of the painted rules. A real paint should be measured, as pnl2's homography does.

**Beat 1:**
- **On phones the cover painting is 9:16, but the clip is 16:9**, played object-fit cover. The first 20% of the scroll cross-fades from the portrait painting into the cropped landscape clip, and the eye sees the marquee jump. A 9:16 clip (start on cover-p, end on a 9:16 ticket frame) fixes it.
- After the ticket fills the screen, the loop begins below with no bridge: the taken ticket is not the one that turns over in step 3. Better: let beat 1 end on the ticket's front, and make step 3 that same ticket.
- "Box office · take a ticket" is my line, not the owner's.

**Beat 2:**
- Four taps is a lot to ask. A visitor who scrolls past sees four closed objects and no facts. Fallback: open each object automatically once it has been fully on screen for 2 s (lesson 55), keeping the tap for impatient readers.
- Ink style: the posters' second state did not light frame 1. The edit came back unchanged, so C in ink relies on its lettering and the flicker to say "lit".
- Style 2: the slip's second state painted two slips.

**Beat 3:**
- On phones the 16:9 cinema is letterboxed over a blurred reflection of itself, which leaves dead space above and below the doors. A 9:16 paint (doors tall) is needed.
- The film has native controls on a small painted screen. At 390 the screen is about 150px wide, so a tap should open it full screen.
- Scrubbing needs Range requests (the domain has them). On an iPhone, setting `currentTime` on scroll is jerkier than in Chrome. Playing the clip once on reaching the section is the safe alternative.

**Data:**
- Every figure follows the sheet, so each line changes with the next round. "GPT closest" is the round's winner (closest call), not the leader.
- The golden ticket shows the flat P&L leader (Grok), as the live site does.
