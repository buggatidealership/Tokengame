# What makes Harry Potter's moving pictures feel magical (helper o, 2026-10-30)

## Findings
- **Ordinary objects, quietly alive.** Photos and portraits are mementos first; the magic is that they keep living. Portraits are the objects "with the most personality"; Dumbledore's names the trick: "paint and memory" (Wizarding World, "How do magical portraits actually work?", 9 Jul 2019).
- **They mind their own business.** Harry looks at his Chocolate Frog card again and Dumbledore has gone: "you can't expect him to hang around all day" (Philosopher's Stone, ch. 6). Nothing performs for you, so noticing feels like a discovery.
- **The object does its own job.** The Marauder's Map shows where everyone is and appears only when asked. MinaLima built it "character driven", lettered from the real plans so it stays true (Eye, 26 Feb 2014). The motion carries information.
- **A living photograph is one living moment in a still.** Beck and Burg coined "cinemagraph" in March 2011: "I think of it as a living photograph" (cinemagraphs.com; Time). One part moves, the rest holds, the seam is invisible.
- **It holds the eye, and steals it.** Eye tracking, 30 people: cinemagraphs held first fixations 1.7 to 2.1 times longer than the same stills, with 26 to 32 revisits against 11 to 14 (Jurič, Tomić, Pál, GRID 2024). Peripheral movement forces a shift of attention; slow fades distract least; many animations cancel out (NN/g, 21 Sep 2014).
- **Calm technology.** Information lives at the periphery and moves to the centre only when needed (Weiser and Brown, 1995).

## Principles for the comic page
1. Life inside the picture, never on the page: frames, words and numbers stay still.
2. One living thing per picture, chosen for character (a creature, a machine at work), not an effect laid on top.
3. Ration motion: at most one thing moving in view, or only when looked at.
4. Slow and seamless: slower than real time, fades not slides, no visible loop seam.
5. Motion that tells something: a moving tile says "there is a film behind me"; a bell causes the grade; wet ink says "just graded".
6. Let it be discovered: no badge, no "live" label.
7. Honest life: wetness from the real clock; light breathes only where a film exists.
8. Stillness on request: reduced motion and off-screen pictures hold still.
