# What hooks people, what gets them to buy — research notes (2026-09-28)

Labels: **[sourced]** = from a cited source · **[derived]** = my inference from sources + this project · **[hypothesis]** = untested, needs real traffic to confirm.

## Findings
1. **Attention and trading move together; durability is the problem.** Memecoin value tracks community activity in real time, and ~97% fail within a year. The durable ones have a community that *produces* things (content, tools, culture), not just watches. [sourced: DeSpread, arXiv 2412.04913, FinancialContent 2026]
2. **Hype era → trust era.** After years of rugs, buyers screen for transparency: verifiable contract, on-chain actions, public numbers. [sourced: Anndy Lian 2025, memecoin checklists]
3. **Buybacks work when they're a live public number.** Hyperliquid's Assistance Fund (≈99% of fees → HYPE buybacks, >$1.3B by Oct 2025) is tracked on public dashboards; the dashboard *is* the marketing. [sourced: CoinShares, crypto.news, assistancefund.top]
4. **Prediction markets spread through their numbers, not their site.** Polymarket's odds get quoted by media and on X — free distribution every time a big event happens. [sourced: indiemarketer, preipomedia] Caveat: WSJ reported staged fake bets; faking the record is the fastest way to lose this audience. [sourced]
5. **Habit = cue → action → variable reward.** Daily-bonus mechanics are the common playbook. [sourced: Appcues, Nir Eyal hook model]
6. **Card backs:** the most important field is the largest; readable at arm's length; don't treat the back as an afterthought. [sourced: Topps history, card-design guides]
7. **Mobile-first:** primary action in the bottom ~40% of the screen, ≥44px tap targets, one idea per screen. [sourced: 2026 mobile UX guides]

## What this means for EZ
- **Don't fake variable rewards — you already have a real one.** Every print is a genuinely unknown outcome. The loop is: countdown (cue) → calls land (action: look) → the grade (variable reward). No streaks or daily chests needed. [derived]
- **Make the record quotable.** Each round card should work as a standalone image/link on X, the way Polymarket odds travel. Per-round links + shareable cards do this. [derived]
- **Give the community something to make.** "Your card" (your own call) is the smallest version of this; a public leaderboard of human calls would be the next — but needs a backend. [hypothesis]
- **Buy conversion comes from a checkable public number.** If the purse and any buybacks are shown live with transaction hashes, that's the Hyperliquid pattern. Today there are no figures to show — until there are, the page shouldn't imply them. [derived]
- **The honesty is a feature.** Showing the Costco card (nobody wins) as prominently as the wins is exactly the trust signal this audience screens for. [derived]

## What would prove this wrong
- People share the films but never visit the page → distribution lives on X; the page should be a thin receipt, not a destination.
- Visits don't spike around print dates → the calendar isn't the cue; something else is.
- Many "your card" makes but no buys → the game entertains but doesn't convert; the purse/on-chain story has to carry that.
