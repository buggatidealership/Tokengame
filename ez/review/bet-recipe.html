The bet section, reverse engineered

Not what each part is, but why it works: the ten decisions behind it, the words to ask for them, and a brief you can fill in for any other section of the page.

The ten decisions

  1. One world for the whole chapter.Everything lives in one place with one logic: money and machines. The vault holds the pool, the chip runs the clock, the circuit runs the mechanism. No element comes from outside that world.Ask for: "one world for this section"
  2. Three panels, three questions, in order.What is at stake (the vault), when (the countdown), how it works (the pool, the call, the switch). Each panel answers one question and hands over to the next.Ask for: "a sequence of beats", "one question per panel"
  3. The first panel sets the scene.Biggest picture first, no data in it, only the stakes and the mood. The title sits on it like a comic cover's logo.Ask for: "an establishing shot"
  4. The live number lives inside the world.The countdown is real, updated every second, but it sits in the chip's own windows. Nothing floats on a box above the picture.Ask for: "diegetic data", "the number lives in the picture"
  5. Later panels call back to earlier ones.Panel 3 is the same circuit board as panel 2, and the text under it sits on the same board's traces. The eye reads one continuous object.Ask for: "a callback", "the panels share one material"
  6. Labels in the world's own language.C1, U1, K1 are the codes engineers print beside parts. DAYS and HRS are printed on the board like silkscreen. The labels feel found, not added.Ask for: "label it the way that world labels things"
  7. One finish everywhere.Painted 3D, sharp, lit by one warm key light, a soft blurred background behind sharp objects, in the logo's black, gold, green and red only.Ask for: "one finish, one light, one palette"
  8. No containers.No cards, no rounded boxes, no flat colour fills drawn by code. The painting is the panel; text sits straight on the world's own ground.Ask for: "no modules, the picture is the module"
  9. Words only label.One title, one caption, three subheads with one line each. The pictures carry the meaning.Ask for: "captions, not paragraphs"
  10. Motion that means something.The coin glints because the pool is gold; the lamps alternate because right or wrong is still open. Nothing moves for decoration.Ask for: "motion that acts out the fact"

The words to use

SayIt means
Establishing shotThe opening picture that shows where we are and what is at stake, with no data in it.
Beat / sequenceOne step of the story; a section is a few beats in a set order.
DiegeticSomething that exists inside the picture's world (a number on the chip), not laid on top of it.
CallbackA later panel reusing an earlier panel's object or material, so the two read as one.
One finishEvery picture rendered the same way (here: painted 3D, sharp, comic ink).
Key lightThe one main light that shapes everything; the warm light from the vault.
Depth of field / bokehSharp objects in front of a soft, blurred background; it makes things feel real and touchable.
PlateA painted picture used as the whole surface of a panel, which the page then writes on.
GroundWhat is behind the text: here the board's own traces, never a flat box.
Hinge captionThe one line between panels that carries you to the next ("It goes on at the close...").
Container / moduleA box drawn around content. The bet section has none; that is why it feels made, not assembled.

The brief, reusable for any section

CHAPTER: [name of the section] THE ONE QUESTION IT ANSWERS: [what a visitor wants to know here] WORLD: [one place, one logic, everything in it belongs there] BEATS (in order, one question each): 1 Establishing shot: [the place and the stakes, no data, the title on it] 2 The live figure: [the number that changes, living inside an object of this world] 3 The mechanism: [how it works, as objects of the same world, a callback to beat 2] LABELS: [the codes or marks this world really uses] FINISH: painted 3D, sharp, one warm key light, depth of field, black, gold, green and red; no flat code fills, no boxes WORDS: one title, one hinge caption per beat, subheads of one line MOTION: [one small movement per beat that acts out a fact] DATA STAYS LIVE: every number and word is page text set into the painted surface; the painting is made empty for it
